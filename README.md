Domain: Pharmacovigilance

### 1\. Abstract

Pharmacovigilance plays an important role in monitoring the safety of medicines and identifying adverse drug reactions (ADRs). Adverse drug reactions can vary in intensity, clinical significance and impact on patient health.

Severity and case classification are important components of pharmacovigilance used to evaluate the clinical impact of adverse drug reactions. Severity assessment determines the intensity of an adverse reaction, whereas seriousness assessment identifies whether the reaction meets specific regulatory criteria, such as death, life-threatening conditions, hospitalisation or permanent disability.

Expectedness assessment determines whether the reported adverse reaction is consistent with the known safety information of the suspected drug.

This project focuses on the classification of adverse drug reactions based on severity, seriousness and expectedness. Different clinical cases are evaluated to determine their clinical impact and importance in medication safety.

### 2\. Introduction

Medicines are widely used for the prevention, diagnosis and treatment of diseases. However, their administration may sometimes produce unwanted and harmful effects known as adverse drug reactions.

ADRs can range from mild symptoms, such as nausea and itching, to severe and life-threatening conditions, such as gastrointestinal haemorrhage and lactic acidosis.

In pharmacovigilance, proper classification of adverse drug reactions is essential to identify high-risk reactions, assess patient safety and support appropriate clinical interventions.

Three important aspects of ADR classification are:

* Severity: Determines the intensity of the adverse reaction.

* Seriousness: Determines the clinical and regulatory significance of the reaction.

* Expectedness: Determines whether the reaction is consistent with the previously documented safety profile of the drug.

### 6\. Classification of Adverse Drug Reactions Based on Severity

Severity refers to the intensity of an adverse drug reaction experienced by a patient.

ADRs are generally classified into three categories.

### A. Mild Adverse Drug Reactions

Mild reactions produce minor symptoms that do not significantly affect the patient's daily activities.

Characteristics:

* Minimal discomfort.

* No significant medical intervention required.

* Usually resolve without specific treatment.

* No significant interruption of normal activities.

Clinical manifestations:

* Mild nausea.

* Headache.

* Mild itching.

* Occasional dizziness.

### B. Moderate Adverse Drug Reactions

Moderate reactions produce noticeable symptoms that may interfere with daily activities and require medical intervention.

Characteristics:

* Moderate discomfort.

* Medical treatment may be required.

* May require modification or discontinuation of the suspected drug.

* May interfere with normal daily activities.

Clinical manifestations:

* Persistent vomiting.

* Moderate skin rashes.

* Significant dizziness.

* Moderate gastrointestinal disturbances.

### C. Severe Adverse Drug Reactions

Severe reactions produce intense symptoms that may significantly affect the patient's health and require intensive medical management.

Characteristics:

* Severe clinical manifestations.

* Significant impairment of normal activities.

* May require emergency medical treatment.

* May be associated with life-threatening complications.

Clinical manifestations:

* Severe gastrointestinal bleeding.

* Severe allergic reactions.

* Lactic acidosis.

* Severe hepatotoxicity.

### 7\. Classification Based on Seriousness

Seriousness is a regulatory classification based on the outcome or consequences of an adverse event.

According to standard pharmacovigilance criteria, an adverse event is considered serious if it results in any of the following:

### A. Death

An adverse reaction that directly results in the death of a patient.

### B. Life-Threatening Condition

An adverse reaction that places the patient at immediate risk of death at the time of the event.

### C. Hospitalisation

An adverse reaction that requires hospital admission or prolongation of an existing hospital stay.

### D. Persistent or Significant Disability

An adverse reaction that results in substantial impairment of normal daily activities or functioning.

### E. Congenital Anomaly

An adverse reaction associated with a birth defect or congenital abnormality.

### F. Medically Important Event

An adverse reaction that may not immediately result in death or hospitalisation but requires medical intervention to prevent serious outcomes.

### Classification

| Category    | Description                                         |
| ----------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| Serious     | Meets at least one regulatory seriousness criterion |
| Non-serious | Does not meet any regulatory seriousness criterion  |

Important: Severe does not automatically mean serious. A reaction can be severe in intensity but non-serious under regulatory criteria. Similarly, a reaction of moderate intensity may be serious if it requires hospitalisation.

### 8\. Classification Based on Expectedness

Expectedness determines whether an adverse drug reaction is consistent with the established safety information of a medicine.

The reported reaction is compared with the applicable approved product information or reference safety information.

### A. Expected Adverse Drug Reactions

Expected reactions are those already documented in the established safety information of the suspected medicine.

Characteristics:

* Previously recognised reactions.

* Included in approved product information.

* Consistent with the known safety profile of the medicine.

Examples:

* Amoxicillin-associated skin rash.

* Ibuprofen-associated gastrointestinal bleeding.

* Metformin-associated lactic acidosis.

### B. Unexpected Adverse Drug Reactions

Unexpected reactions are those whose nature, specificity or severity is inconsistent with the applicable established safety information.

Characteristics:

* Not documented in the applicable reference safety information.

* Different from the known reaction profile.

* May indicate a new or previously unrecognised safety concern.

Importance:

Unexpected adverse drug reactions require careful evaluation and appropriate pharmacovigilance reporting.

### 9\. Assessment Criteria

### A. Patient Information

Collection of relevant demographic details, medical history and clinical condition.

### B. Suspected Drug Information

Identification of the suspected medicine, dosage, route of administration and duration of treatment.

### C. Adverse Reaction Identification

Documentation of the type, onset, duration and clinical manifestations of the reported reaction.

### D. Severity Assessment

Evaluation of the intensity of symptoms and their effect on the patient's normal activities.

### E. Seriousness Assessment

Evaluation of whether the adverse reaction resulted in death, hospitalisation, disability, life-threatening conditions or other medically important events.

### F. Expectedness Assessment

Comparison of the reported adverse reaction with the established safety information of the suspected medicine.

### G. Clinical Outcome

Assessment of recovery, improvement, persistence or worsening of the adverse reaction.

### 10\. Clinical Case Studies and Classification

### Case Study 1: Amoxicillin-Induced Skin Rash and Itching

Non-serious case

Patient details: 45-year-old female.

Suspected drug: Amoxicillin.

Adverse reaction: Skin rash and itching.

Clinical presentation: The patient developed skin rash and itching following administration of amoxicillin.

Severity assessment: Mild to moderate, depending on the extent of rash and symptoms.

Seriousness assessment: Non-serious, provided there is no hospitalisation, life-threatening condition or other seriousness criterion.

Expectedness assessment: Expected if the reaction is documented in the applicable approved product information.

Clinical interpretation: Amoxicillin may cause hypersensitivity reactions due to immune-mediated mechanisms. Skin rash and itching are recognised manifestations of drug hypersensitivity.

### Case Study 2: Ibuprofen-Induced Gastrointestinal Bleeding

Serious case

Patient details: 50-year-old male.

Suspected drug: Ibuprofen.

Adverse reaction: Gastrointestinal bleeding requiring hospitalisation.

Clinical presentation: The patient developed gastrointestinal bleeding following ibuprofen administration and required hospitalisation.

Severity assessment: Severe.

Seriousness assessment: Serious because the reaction required hospitalisation.

Expectedness assessment: Expected if gastrointestinal bleeding is documented in the applicable approved product information.

Clinical interpretation: Ibuprofen is a non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID). It inhibits COX-1 and COX-2 enzymes, reducing prostaglandin synthesis. Decreased protective prostaglandins in the gastrointestinal mucosa can increase the risk of ulceration and gastrointestinal bleeding.

### Case Study 3: Metformin-Associated Lactic Acidosis

Serious and potentially life-threatening case

Patient details: 62-year-old female.

Suspected drug: Metformin.

Adverse reaction: Lactic acidosis.

Clinical presentation: The patient developed lactic acidosis during metformin treatment.

Severity assessment: Severe.

Seriousness assessment: Serious because lactic acidosis can be life-threatening and may require hospitalisation.

Expectedness assessment: Expected if the reaction is consistent with the applicable approved product information.

Clinical interpretation: Metformin reduces hepatic gluconeogenesis and decreases hepatic glucose production. In susceptible patients, particularly those with significant renal impairment, drug accumulation and associated metabolic disturbances can contribute to lactic acidosis.

The assessment of adverse drug reactions demonstrates that different medicines can produce reactions with varying degrees of severity and clinical significance.

Ibuprofen-associated gastrointestinal bleeding requiring hospitalisation is classified as a serious adverse event because it meets the hospitalisation criterion.

### 14\. Limitations

* ADR classification depends on the accuracy and completeness of clinical information.

* Severity may vary between individual patients.

* Seriousness depends on the clinical outcome and regulatory criteria.

* Expectedness must be assessed against the applicable reference safety information.

* Some adverse reactions may have multiple contributing factors.

* Clinical judgement is essential for accurate classification.
